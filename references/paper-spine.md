# Paper spine: question, answer, evidence, contribution

Use for whole-paper organization, contribution diagnosis, or major restructuring.
A spine is a working account of the argument, not a required manuscript section.

## Reconstruct the existing argument first

Identify:
- The precise question and why its answer matters.
- The answer the paper currently supports.
- The nearest literature or competing account, within sources actually inspected.
- What evidence or reasoning makes the answer credible, and its limits.
- What changes in understanding because of this answer.

Do not require all papers to follow "gap → directional hypothesis → regression".
A theoretical proof, descriptive measurement, predictive evaluation, qualitative
process explanation or synthesis can carry the central answer. In a proposal the
answer is unknown: record the question and planned evidence, not a fictitious finding.

## Test the contribution

Distinguish:
- **Stated contribution:** what the introduction promises.
- **Supported increment:** what the actual analysis adds.
- **Unresolved claim:** what would require more literature or research.

A new setting/data source can matter if it changes measurement, tests a boundary,
reveals a process or resolves a substantive uncertainty. Explain that consequence.
Do not automatically label it a theoretical contribution or dismiss it as worthless.
Using an existing estimator is not by itself a methodological innovation.
A search that finds no close paper does not prove global novelty.

## Keep a compact mapping when useful

| Claim or answer | Evidence and locator | Allowed interpretation | Limit or open question |
|---|---|---|---|
| The author's actual statement | Table/column, proposition/proof, interview passage, or source section | What this material establishes | Scope, alternative, missing material |

Use actual evidence, including nulls and contradictions. Statistical stars are
not a confidence rating for the scientific claim. For numbers use
[results-to-text](results-to-text.md); for sources use
[evidence-grading](evidence-grading.md).

A one-paragraph diagnosis may suffice. Do not produce a table, risk register and
revision matrix containing the same issue. Persist a mapping only if it helps
long-form or repeated work.

## Repair the weakest consequential link

Locate the defect before prescribing work:
- Unclear question → narrow the object or comparison.
- Vague contribution → compare the supported increment with the closest work.
- Construct/proxy mismatch → clarify measurement and narrow or validate the claim.
- Evidence gap → distinguish reporting omission, missing analysis and unavailable data.
- Overreach → offer supported wording; discuss new research only if it is worth doing.
- Contradictory evidence → explain the conditions or acknowledge the unresolved result.

No rule requires adding a mechanism test, new theory, IV or heterogeneity analysis.
Use [topic advice](topic-revision-advisor.md) and
[theory reasoning](theory-backing-router.md) for consequential choices.

## Propagate a substantive change

When a core estimate or answer changes, inspect affected title/abstract, introduction,
hypotheses or predictions, results, discussion, conclusion and reviewer responses.
Use the claim's locations to target edits. Different samples and specifications may
correctly give different estimates; do not enforce numeric uniformity.

Deliver the central diagnosis, located breaks and a feasible repair order. State
what already works. Distinguish editorial repair from work requiring new evidence.

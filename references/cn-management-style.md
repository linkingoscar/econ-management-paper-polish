# Chinese Management Style

Use for Chinese management papers, CSSCI management journals, Chinese strategy/organization/innovation/HR/marketing/IS/operations/public management manuscripts, and Chinese firm-level empirical papers.

## Core Orientation

Chinese management papers usually need both problem consciousness and theory contribution. The writing should connect Chinese managerial or institutional phenomena to constructs, mechanisms, and actionable implications.

Default logic:

```text
管理/组织现象 -> 理论问题 -> 文献不足 -> 构念与机制 -> 假设/研究设计 -> 发现 -> 理论贡献 -> 管理启示
```

## Introduction

Strong Chinese management introductions:

- Use a concrete organizational, strategic, governance, innovation, HR, platform, or consumer phenomenon.
- Move quickly to a theoretical question.
- Define core constructs early.
- Explain why existing theories or empirical findings are incomplete.
- State mechanisms and boundary conditions when relevant.
- Present contribution to named literature streams.

Avoid:

- Treating "中国情境" as contribution by itself.
- Policy-style opening that never becomes a management theory problem.
- Empty "理论意义和实践意义" paragraphs.

## Theory And Hypotheses

Management theory sections need more logic than economics-style empirical papers:

- Define each construct.
- Identify theoretical lens.
- Explain mechanism.
- Acknowledge competing or countervailing logic.
- Derive directional hypotheses.
- Explain moderators as boundary conditions, not post-hoc subgroups.

Hypotheses should be concise but not merely restated regression expectations.

## Empirical Sections

For archival management papers:

- Clarify sample, variables, controls, fixed effects, endogeneity concerns, robustness.
- Return results to theory and mechanisms.

For survey/experiment papers:

- Clarify construct validity, scale reliability, sampling, manipulation, common-method concerns, and causal limits.

## Discussion And Implications

Separate:

- Theoretical contribution: what concept, theory, mechanism, or boundary condition is advanced.
- Management implications: what managers, organizations, platforms, or policymakers can do, within evidence scope.
- Limitations and future research.

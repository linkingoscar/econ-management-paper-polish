# English Economics Style

Use for English economics papers, applied micro papers, economics field-journal submissions, and economics-style finance/IS/platform papers.

## Core Orientation

English economics writing prioritizes a precise research question, credible identification, economic mechanism, magnitude, and contribution to an identifiable literature.

Default logic:

```text
Question/puzzle -> why hard to answer -> setting/data/variation -> empirical design -> findings/magnitude -> mechanism/robustness -> contribution
```

## Introduction

A strong English economics introduction:

- Opens with a sharp question or puzzle, not a broad slogan.
- Explains why the answer is not obvious.
- Presents the setting as useful for identification or theory, not merely as a local context.
- States the identifying variation early.
- Reports main results with economic magnitude when possible.
- Ends with contributions mapped to specific literatures.

Avoid:

- "This topic is important because..." without a puzzle.
- Long institutional background before the research question.
- Overstating causal claims before design is established.

## Theory And Mechanism

In many applied economics papers, theory should be concise:

- State mechanism intuition.
- Derive empirical implication.
- Mention countervailing forces or alternative explanations.
- Avoid long hypothesis sections unless the target field expects them.

Use "testable prediction", "empirical implication", or "mechanism" more often than management-style "Hypothesis 1" when appropriate.

## Empirical Design

English economics reviewers ask:

- What identifies the effect?
- Why is the variation plausibly exogenous?
- What assumptions are required?
- What would invalidate the design?
- What is the economic magnitude?

Write methods in terms of design, not only equations.

## Results

Results paragraphs should:

- State estimate, sign, and magnitude.
- Interpret economic meaning.
- Relate to identifying variation.
- Address alternative explanations with specific checks.

Do not overuse "robust"; state what changed and what remained stable.

## China Or Emerging-Market Context

When translating a China-based paper into English:

- Explain why the setting is theoretically or empirically useful.
- Avoid excessive policy slogans.
- Translate institutions functionally, not literally, when needed.
- Make the paper legible to readers outside China.

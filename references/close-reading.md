# Focused reading and critical synthesis

Use for detailed analysis of a paper or extracting evidence for a specific claim.
Start with the user's question and available source content; no fixed reading time
or universal template is required.

## Record what is actually known

For a substantial reading note, include the useful parts:
- Source/version and material inspected.
- Research question and the author's claimed contribution.
- Approach, key constructs and evidence or proof.
- Main findings with actual locators and interpretation limits.
- Your assessment, distinguished from the author's statements.
- Relevance to the user's claim or study and unresolved checks.

Skip inapplicable fields. A qualitative paper need not supply a coefficient table;
a theory paper need not have a sample; an abstract-only task cannot yield a complete
methods/results audit.

## Read the argument critically

Trace what connects the question to the answer. Compare claims with methods,
tables, proofs, passages or observations available.
Preserve contradictory and null findings and distinguish:
author's explanation; observed evidence; your inference; remaining alternative.

For empirical results use [results-to-text](results-to-text.md).
For source support use [evidence-grading](evidence-grading.md).
Use rendered pages when extraction obscures important numbers, equations or figures.
Do not fabricate quotes, page locators, coding steps, mechanism tests or proof checks.

References cited by this paper are leads until independently inspected.
Citation counts or forward-citation relationships require actual retrieval;
do not invent a citation network from memory.

## Deliver

Explain what the paper establishes, its consequential limits, and how it can
inform the user's task. Include short quotes only when useful and allowed;
otherwise paraphrase with locators. A few focused notes can be more useful than
a long generic checklist. Persist notes only when requested or valuable for reuse.

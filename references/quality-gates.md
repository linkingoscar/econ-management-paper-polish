# Quality Gates

Run these checks when reviewing, rewriting substantively, or polishing sensitive sections.

## Paradigm Fit Gate

Before polishing, check whether the revision matches the routed discipline:

- Economics: does the text foreground research question, identification, institutional setting, and economic magnitude rather than generic management implications?
- Management/organization: does the text develop constructs, mechanisms, boundary conditions, and theory contribution rather than only reporting empirical associations?
- Finance/accounting: does the text handle institutional details, proxy validity, endogeneity, market participants, and economic interpretation precisely?
- Marketing/IS: does the text connect the finding to consumer/firm/platform/technology mechanisms rather than treating the context as generic business behavior?
- Applied public/tourism/regional fields: are policy or sector implications scoped to the studied setting?

If the draft mixes paradigms, revise toward the user's target journal or ask for the target outlet.

## Journal Fit Gate

If a target journal is named:

- Do not assume current author requirements from memory.
- Check current author instructions and recent papers when the task needs journal-specific formatting or style.
- If current requirements are not checked, label the revision as "general style adaptation" rather than journal-specific compliance.

For Chinese CSSCI-compatible writing:

- Is the problem consciousness explicit?
- Is the literature gap specific?
- Are possible marginal contributions connected to named literature streams?
- Are policy or managerial implications evidence-bounded?

For English field-journal-compatible writing:

- Is the prose claim-first and concise?
- Are contributions framed through literature, theory, identification, or method rather than generic importance?
- Are Chinese-context details translated into internationally legible institutional or theoretical language?

## Claim-Evidence Gate

For each claim, ask:

- Is it theoretical, empirical, methodological, managerial, or policy-oriented?
- Is there a citation, model, table, figure, or institutional fact supporting it?
- Does the strength of language match the evidence?
- Is the claim too broad for the sample, period, or setting?

Flag unsupported claims rather than making them sound smoother.

## Citation Gate

Never create fake references. If a citation is missing, mark `[citation needed]`.

Check for:

- Source access has been classified when access is uncertain; use `source-access-policy.md`.
- Any new or replacement reference has a traceable current-session source: DOI, publisher page, CNKI/official database page, Zotero item, Crossref/OpenAlex result, official document, or user-provided file.
- Citation supports the specific claim, not just the general topic.
- Seminal and recent literature are not conflated.
- "First", "novel", and "to our knowledge" claims are not made without search evidence.
- Chinese and English sources are not mixed in a way that changes terminology or construct meaning.
- Reference metadata is complete enough for the requested style. Default is APA 7.
- New or replacement references meet the minimum evidence grade from `evidence-grading.md`.

If verification is not possible, do not cite. Provide a candidate list for user confirmation instead.

## Source Access Gate

When literature, methods, policy facts, target-journal style, topic/frontier claims, or variable suggestions depend on external sources:

- State whether sources were full-text verified, metadata/abstract verified, public-source preliminary, candidate only, or access limited.
- Downgrade confidence when only metadata, abstracts, search results, or secondary summaries were available.
- Do not claim systematic coverage unless a systematic search strategy and source set were actually used.
- Do not label a direction mainstream/frontier without current-session evidence from appropriate source channels.
- If access is blocked, provide a retrieval plan rather than filling gaps with unsupported claims.

## Numbers And Tables Gate

Before finalizing result text:

- Match every coefficient sign with the table.
- Match p-values, stars, sample size, and column numbers.
- Distinguish statistical significance from economic significance.
- Avoid interpreting log coefficients, interaction terms, DID estimates, or standardized variables incorrectly.
- For interaction terms, interpret marginal effects only when the model supports the interpretation.

## Identification And Causal Language Gate

Allowed language depends on design:

- RCT/natural experiment/credible DID/RD/IV: causal language may be appropriate if assumptions are stated.
- Panel FE, matching, cross-sectional regression: prefer association/evidence language unless identification is strong.
- Mechanism and heterogeneity tests: avoid claiming proof; use "consistent with".

Red flags:

- "solves endogeneity" from ordinary controls.
- "causes" in a purely correlational design.
- "mechanism is verified" without a direct mechanism proxy.
- "robustness proves" instead of "results are consistent across".

## Method Currency Gate

When the user asks for advanced/current empirical methods:

- Search current method literature or recent target-field papers before naming specific "advanced" estimators as authoritative.
- Explain why the method fits the data and identification problem.
- Distinguish a robustness check, an identification strategy, a measurement method, and an estimator.
- Do not upgrade methods in prose if the manuscript's actual design and data cannot support them.

## Section-Specific Gate

Introduction:

- Is there a clear puzzle or research question?
- Are contributions specific and literature-linked?
- Are main findings concrete?

Theory:

- Does each hypothesis follow from a mechanism?
- Are competing mechanisms acknowledged when relevant?

Empirical strategy:

- Are sample, variables, fixed effects, controls, and standard errors clear?
- Is the identifying variation explained?

Results:

- Are signs, magnitudes, and implications stated?
- Does each result map back to a hypothesis or research question?

Discussion:

- Are implications scoped to the evidence?
- Are limitations honest and not self-defeating?

## Reviewer-Risk Gate

Flag issues likely to trigger reviewer criticism:

- Contribution is framed as "fills a gap" but the gap is not theoretically meaningful.
- Literature review lists papers without positioning.
- Hypotheses are obvious or not falsifiable.
- Identification threat is mentioned but not addressed.
- Mechanism proxy is weak or endogenous.
- Heterogeneity is exploratory but presented as theory-confirming.
- Managerial implications exceed the data.

## Topic And Revision Advice Gate

When recommending a new topic, variable design, or revision direction:

- Has the current manuscript weakness been diagnosed first?
- Is the suggestion a conservative revision, an ambitious pivot, or a new-paper idea?
- Does the suggestion fit the routed discipline, language, and target outlet?
- Are variable suggestions feasible with the user's data and timing?
- Is each direction supported by traceable literature or clearly labeled as speculative?
- Are mainstream/frontier claims backed by current-session evidence?
- Are reviewer risks stated for each recommended path?

# Style And Polish

## Chinese Academic Prose

Prefer precise, compact academic Chinese:

- First decide whether the paper targets economics, management, finance/accounting, marketing, IS, or another applied field. Do not use the same "现实意义-理论意义-管理启示" template for all fields.
- Use "本文考察/检验/识别/揭示/发现" according to evidence strength.
- Use "结果表明" for empirical findings; use "机制检验显示" only when a mechanism is actually tested.
- Replace vague intensifiers with concrete scope, mechanism, or evidence.
- Keep long sentences under control. If a sentence carries background, mechanism, and finding at once, split it.
- Use consistent terms for variables, constructs, firms, regions, and institutions.

Common weak phrases to revise:

- "具有重要意义" -> specify theoretical, empirical, managerial, or policy relevance.
- "产生一定影响" -> state direction and channel if known.
- "在一定程度上" -> remove or specify the condition.
- "丰富了相关研究" -> name the literature stream and explain the incremental contribution.
- "提供了经验证据" -> state evidence for what proposition.
- "进一步地" repeated mechanically -> use logical transitions such as "机制上", "识别上", "异质性分析显示".

## English Academic Prose

Prefer direct journal-style English:

- First decide whether the paper follows economics-style identification writing, management-style theory writing, finance/accounting institutional writing, marketing decision-oriented writing, or IS sociotechnical writing.
- Use "examines", "tests", "documents", "shows", "provides evidence" with evidence-appropriate strength.
- Avoid "proves" unless the paper is formal theory.
- Avoid inflated adjectives: novel, significant, substantial, comprehensive, important, and robust unless made concrete.
- Lead paragraphs with claims, not background throat-clearing.
- Use active voice where it improves clarity.

Common replacements:

- "This paper makes an important contribution" -> "This paper contributes to [literature] by showing [specific result]."
- "The results are robust" -> "The estimate remains similar after [specific check]."
- "To the best of our knowledge" -> use sparingly and only after citation checks.
- "May have a significant impact" -> "is associated with [directional outcome]" or "is expected to [mechanism]" depending on evidence.

## AI-Tone Reduction

Remove generic, template-like academic filler:

- Broad opening slogans.
- Repeated "first, second, third" without conceptual distinction.
- Overbalanced sentences with no real claim.
- Empty contribution paragraphs.
- Excessive hedging and repetitive transition words.

Make prose look human and discipline-specific by adding:

- Concrete constructs.
- Specific institutional setting.
- Exact sample/time frame.
- Named identification challenge.
- Mechanism-specific language.
- Table/column references when discussing results.

## Line-Editing Priorities

When polishing, improve in this order:

1. Meaning preservation.
2. Claim precision.
3. Logical order.
4. Sentence economy.
5. Academic tone.
6. Terminology consistency.

Do not make prose ornate. Good econ/management writing is clear, testable, and restrained.

## Translation Rules

For Chinese to English:

- Translate the argument, not each clause.
- Convert Chinese topic-heavy sentences into English claim-first sentences.
- Convert China-specific policy or institutional background into internationally legible motivation when the target is an English journal.
- Preserve variable names, citation keys, table numbers, and model notation.
- Use "we" only if the target journal allows it or the user's draft already uses it.

For English to Chinese:

- Avoid literal calques.
- Keep discipline terms stable.
- Preserve causal strength.
- Adapt to Chinese academic section logic when requested, especially problem consciousness, literature gap, and scoped implications.
- Avoid officialese unless the target is a Chinese journal that expects it.

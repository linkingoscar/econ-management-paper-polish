# Faithful editing and translation

Use for polishing, translation, shortening and paragraph restructuring.
Deliver the requested revision first. An already clear passage may be retained.

## Before changing words

Identify the passage's job: define, motivate, compare literature, explain a design,
report evidence, infer, qualify, or connect sections. Improve that job at the
requested depth; do not silently change it.

Protect two kinds of content:

- **Exact anchors:** numbers and signs, units, dates, sample sizes, variable names,
  equation symbols, citation keys, table/column references and technical terms.
  Keep retained details exact. Ordinary line edits preserve them; substantive
  rewriting may select among them when omission does not distort the argument.
- **Meaning:** who makes a claim, what is compared, direction, negation, population,
  time, uncertainty, causal status, conditions and exceptions. Citation attachment
  belongs to meaning, not just to the list of unchanged keys.

An unchanged number can acquire a false meaning after a subject, unit or denominator
changes. A sentence can preserve every citation yet attribute a result to the wrong
paper. Check both anchors and meaning.

## Edit at the requested depth

For line edits, repair ambiguity and repetition using concrete subjects and verbs;
retain necessary detail and qualifications. Do not turn a local edit into an
argument overhaul.

For substantive rewriting or compression, first formulate the passage's most
informative answer in manuscript language. Try it as an opening; place context
before it only when readers need that context to understand the question or process.
"The cases differ" and "three mechanisms matter" usually name a subject without
giving the answer. Select evidence that earns the answer, including counterevidence;
do not keep a detail merely because it occupied a sentence in the draft. Compress
background and repeated conclusions to make room for the difficult inference.

Where an abstract label is doing the explanatory work, try rewriting that paragraph
without the label. Show whose interpretation, choice or responsibilities change,
and why. Restore useful terminology once the reasoning is intelligible. If the
needed premise is missing from the notes, inspect the relevant passage in an
original source supplied for this task. Read for explanations as well as factual
checks. Narrow or flag an unsupported account rather than completing it by invention.

Constructed editing example, not evidence for any manuscript:
"The consortium sets common learning targets, and schools choose classroom
activities. These arrangements combine coordination and autonomy."
can be developed into:
"The consortium fixes what pupils should be able to do at the end of each unit;
schools choose the activities used to get there. Coordination concerns the expected
outcome, while discretion concerns how it is pursued."
The revision explains the division of decisions instead of repeating its labels.
It does not establish that the arrangement improves learning. Transfer the editing
move, not these facts or this paragraph shape.

Before delivery, try a replacement for the weakest consequential paragraph and
compare it with the draft: does it explain a missing step or remove an obstacle to
understanding? Keep the stronger prose, not both versions or a diagnosis in its
place. Read the opening and ending together; replace a repeated inventory with the
consequence earned by the passage, or stop earlier if the answer is complete.

Preserve citation scope and each statement's evidential role when reordering.
Explain a change to the author's substantive claim; selecting relevant facts from
source notes or trimming repetition needs no change log. Do not invent a mechanism,
fact, result or citation to bridge a gap.
Read the revision against the source for substantive drift.

If source statements conflict, identify the conflict and preserve unresolved values
until evidence resolves it. Correct a clear transcription error only against a
specified source; disclose a material correction. A local style request does not
authorize selecting a preferred scientific conclusion.

## Claim strength and honest uncertainty

Do not mechanically replace verbs on a universal ladder: "documents", "shows",
"identifies", "suggests", "证明" and "揭示" depend on the sentence and research approach.
A proven theoretical proposition need not be weakened to speculation.
An observational relationship must not become an identified causal effect.

Preserve null and mixed results. "Not statistically distinguishable from zero"
does not mean "no effect"; "not ruled out" does not mean "supported."
Keep substantive hedges such as "in this sample" and "conditional on" even when
removing empty filler. Do not strengthen a contribution to satisfy a request for
top-journal tone. Flag a problem or separate a supported substantive alternative.

Write the claim at its defensible scope first, rather than making a broad claim
and then repeatedly retracting it. An outcome's precise name or a local qualifying
clause can carry a limit; retain an explicit explanation when the reader might
otherwise draw a consequentially different inference. Caution should clarify the
answer, not become a second running commentary on the manuscript.

## Chinese and English expression

- In Chinese, replace empty significance claims with the actual question or
  contribution if known. Do not supply missing content merely to avoid
  "在一定程度上" or "具有重要意义".
- In English, use clear subjects, restrained claims and natural transitions.
  Active/passive voice, first person and sentence length are contextual choices.
- Keep the author's established voice and specialized terminology where clear.
  Avoid forced synonyms for constructs, treatments and outcomes.
- Treat "降 AI 味" as removing generic repetition and improving specificity using
  known facts. Do not promise detector avoidance or invent a measurable AI score.

## Translation

Translate the argument with its evidential force, not clause order. Preserve
citation placement, attribution, technical distinctions and numerical meaning.
Use an established translation for a construct; flag consequential ambiguity.
Explain institutional terms briefly only from supplied or verified information.
Do not turn a local institution into a supposedly equivalent foreign institution.

Example (constructed solely to illustrate preservation):
"在本样本中，X 与 Y 负相关，但这一关系在加入行业趋势后不再显著。"
can become
"In this sample, X is negatively associated with Y, but the association is no
longer statistically significant after industry trends are included."
It cannot become "X reduces Y" or "the effect is robust."

## Delivery and final check

Return revised text, with brief notes only for meaningful choices or unresolved
issues. Do not impose a change count or a fixed number of alternatives.
Compare the source and revision for numbers/units, attribution, negation, scope
and claim strength; a text-diff helper can locate changes but cannot certify meaning.
When the user requests only revised text, omit routine explanations.

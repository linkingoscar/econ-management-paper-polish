# Chinese Economics Style

Use for Chinese economics papers, especially CSSCI-style manuscripts, Chinese policy-oriented empirical papers, and Chinese journal submissions in applied economics.

## Core Orientation

Chinese economics writing usually needs to connect policy or institutional background to an academic question quickly. It should not stay at policy commentary.

Default logic:

```text
现实/政策背景 -> 学术问题 -> 文献不足 -> 研究设计 -> 主要发现 -> 边际贡献 -> 政策启示
```

## Introduction

Strong Chinese economics introductions usually:

- Start from a real economic phenomenon, reform, policy, or institutional change.
- Turn the phenomenon into a precise research question.
- Explain why existing literature has not answered it sufficiently.
- State data, identification, model, and main results clearly.
- Frame contribution as literature contribution, method/data contribution, or China-specific institutional contribution.

Avoid:

- Long policy background with no research puzzle.
- "具有重要现实意义" without an academic problem.
- Contributions that only say "丰富了相关研究".

## Literature Review

Use thematic synthesis:

- Policy/event literature.
- Mechanism or theory literature.
- Method or measurement literature.
- Closely related empirical evidence.

End with what the paper adds. Do not merely list domestic and foreign studies.

## Empirical Strategy

Chinese economics journals often expect clear model, variable, sample, and robustness descriptions. High-level CSSCI outlets increasingly expect credible identification.

Must clarify:

- Sample construction and data source.
- Variable definitions and descriptive statistics.
- Baseline model and fixed effects.
- Identification logic or causal-language limits.
- Robustness, endogeneity, mechanism, and heterogeneity checks.

Use "本文识别/检验/考察" according to evidence strength. Avoid saying "解决内生性" unless the design supports it.

## Results

Preferred pattern:

```text
表X报告了基准回归结果。第X列显示，核心解释变量的系数为...，在...水平上显著。这表明...。从经济含义看，...。该结果支持/印证了...
```

Every result paragraph should include table reference, sign, significance, magnitude if available, and interpretation.

## Policy Implications

Policy implications should be:

- Derived from findings, not from generic policy slogans.
- Scoped to sample, period, industry, region, or institution.
- Separated from theoretical contribution.

Use "政策启示" for economics and public policy; use "管理启示" only when firm decision-making is central.
